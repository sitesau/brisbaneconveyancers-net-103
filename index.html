The Fee Components Worth Comparing

Routine professional fees are typically a few hundred to around a thousand dollars, and many solicitors offer a fixed fee confirmed in writing. The variables that change the total live elsewhere, so ask about each one directly:

  • Pre signing contract review: check whether reading the contract before you sign is included or billed separately.
  • Searches: ask which title and property searches are expected on your file and who pays for them.
  • Transfer duty: confirm the solicitor will calculate it and arrange payment; the amount varies by property and eligibility.
  • PEXA settlement: confirm electronic settlement administration is part of the quoted work, not an add on.
  • Excluded work: get any exclusions listed in writing before you commit, so the fixed fee cannot shift later.

Because disbursements are set out separately and vary by property, a cheaper headline fee can still end up dearer once searches and registration charges land.

How Timing Changes the Brief

Most Queensland residential contracts carry a 5 business day cooling off period starting when you receive the contract signed by both parties. Ending the contract during that window can bring a penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price, which makes early review important where finance dates, special conditions or searches may affect whether you proceed.

Auction files need action earlier. There is no cooling off period on property bought at auction, so the contract should be reviewed before you bid, not after. Off the plan purchases carry their own complications, including sunset clauses and disclosure rules that apply when you buy before construction is finished.

If your main concern is the contract itself, compare that specific need with a dedicated contract review service rather than assuming a full settlement brief is required on day one.

Matter Types Need Different Questions

On a purchase, ask who is responsible for each step and when advice arrives, particularly if lender involvement or search results could change your decision. On a sale, document readiness can matter as much as the fee: the mandatory Form 2 seller disclosure and contract preparation shape how quickly negotiations can move once a buyer appears.

First home buyers carry an added layer. Solicitors can guide clients through transfer duty concessions, the First Home Owner Grant and the steps to settlement. The Queensland First Home Owner Grant is currently $30,000 for an eligible new home valued under $750,000, for contracts signed between 20 November 2023 and 30 June 2026, and is scheduled to revert to $15,000 from 1 July 2026. Treat that figure as a starting point for eligibility checks, not a budget assumption.

Commercial matters involving leases, GST and due diligence, and family title transfers without an open market sale, both need scoped questions about what documents are required before a solicitor can confirm the file is routine.
